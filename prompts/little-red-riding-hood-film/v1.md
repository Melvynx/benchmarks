# Little Red Riding Hood — An Illustrated Browser Film

## Mission

Create a complete, authored short film of **Little Red Riding Hood** that plays inside a browser. The finished work must feel illustrated, directed, animated, edited, and emotionally paced—not like a slideshow, storyboard, scrolling page, landing page, or sequence of cards.

Tell the entire fixed story below from beginning to end as one continuous cinematic experience. The film must begin automatically when the document opens and remain beautiful when embedded in a visual comparison frame.

## Fixed format

- Exact film duration: **112,000 milliseconds**.
- Exactly **12 named sequences**, in the order and time ranges below.
- Default seed: **1812**.
- Default playback: autoplay from 0 ms at 1× speed, once, then hold on the final composed frame.
- The first frame must already be a finished visual composition. Do not show a loading screen, start gate, instructions, or blank title card.
- No click, focus, key press, pointer movement, splash dismissal, or iframe interaction may be required to start or continue playback.

## Fixed adaptation and sequence timing

Use this family-friendly rescue adaptation consistently. Keep dialogue sparse and let images, staging, movement, expression, weather, light, and editing tell the story.

### 1. The Basket — 0 to 9,000 ms

Morning in a small village kitchen. Red's mother closes a woven basket containing bread, butter, and a small jar. She fastens Red's hood and asks her to take the basket to her sick grandmother beyond the forest.

Required spoken or captioned line: **“Stay on the path, and do not linger.”**

### 2. The Red Departure — 9,000 to 18,000 ms

Red leaves the warm village. Establish her small figure, the red hood, the basket, the distant tree line, and the path connecting home to the unknown forest.

### 3. The Forest Threshold — 18,000 to 27,000 ms

The forest changes scale around Red. The path narrows, light becomes patterned, and ordinary branches begin to resemble watchful forms. Red continues walking; the environment must be alive within the shot.

### 4. The Wolf Appears — 27,000 to 38,000 ms

The wolf emerges gradually rather than popping into frame. Stage a real encounter with eyelines and shot/reverse-shot logic. He is charming, controlled, and unsettling rather than immediately monstrous.

Required line: **“Good morning, little traveler. Where does this path take you?”**

### 5. The Question — 38,000 to 47,000 ms

Red reveals that she is visiting her grandmother at the cottage beneath three old oak trees. The wolf notices the basket, studies the route, and directs Red's attention toward wildflowers away from the path.

### 6. The Race — 47,000 to 57,000 ms

Intercut two simultaneous actions: Red gathering flowers in a bright clearing, and the wolf racing through a darker shortcut. The cutting rhythm must accelerate while preserving where each character is going.

### 7. At Grandmother's Door — 57,000 to 66,000 ms

The wolf reaches the cottage, imitates Red's knock, and enters. Show the danger through framing, shadows, objects, and reaction—not gore. Grandmother is trapped out of sight but remains alive.

### 8. The Disguise — 66,000 to 75,000 ms

Inside the cottage, the wolf constructs the disguise using Grandmother's nightcap, spectacles, quilt, and bed. Cross-cut with Red approaching through the trees so the audience understands the trap before she does.

### 9. Red Arrives — 75,000 to 84,000 ms

Red enters the unnaturally quiet cottage. The basket, flowers, half-open door, bed curtain, and altered quality of light should build suspense. She approaches the bed.

### 10. What Big… — 84,000 to 95,000 ms

Build the iconic exchange through progressively closer compositions. Include these exact lines in order:

- **“Grandmother, what big eyes you have.”**
- **“All the better to see you with.”**
- **“Grandmother, what big ears you have.”**
- **“All the better to hear you with.”**
- **“Grandmother, what big teeth you have.”**
- **“All the better to eat you with!”**

### 11. The Rescue — 95,000 to 104,000 ms

The wolf reveals himself and lunges. A passing woodsman hears the crash and enters. Resolve the danger with fast, legible, non-graphic action: Red escapes, Grandmother is freed from her hiding place, and the wolf flees through the window back into the forest.

### 12. The Path Home — 104,000 to 112,000 ms

At dawn, Grandmother, Red, and the woodsman stand outside the cottage. Red returns to the path carrying the now-light basket. End on a visual echo of the departure shot, transformed by what happened, then hold the final frame.

## Character continuity bible

Choose the illustration style yourself, but preserve these readable identifiers across every relevant shot:

- **Red:** childlike scale, unmistakable red triangular hood and cape, dark blue or charcoal clothing, oval woven basket.
- **Wolf:** long narrow muzzle, dark silver coat, amber eyes, one notch in the left ear, physically larger than Red.
- **Grandmother:** silver hair gathered high, round spectacles, a quilt with a repeated botanical motif.
- **Mother:** white apron and a small red ribbon echoing Red's hood.
- **Woodsman:** broad ochre outerwear and a recognizable axe silhouette used as a protective emblem, never for graphic violence.

Characters must not randomly change proportions, costume, handedness, facial structure, or identifying marks between shots. The cottage exterior and interior must also preserve spatial relationships.

## Cinematic and illustration requirements

- Give every sequence a distinct full-frame composition; do not reuse one stage with palette swaps.
- Use at least four clearly different shot scales across the film: extreme wide, wide, medium, close-up, or extreme close-up.
- Use motivated camera behavior such as tracking, push-in, pan, parallax, rack-like focus, or handheld tension. Do not simply zoom the entire page.
- Include at least one match cut, one reveal motivated by occlusion or light, and the required parallel editing during **The Race**.
- Animate within shots: walking cycles, breathing, fabric, hair, foliage, weather, shadows, gaze, hands, props, and environmental response. A crossfade between static illustrations is not sufficient.
- Maintain screen direction, eyelines, entrances, exits, and geography so the film can be understood without captions.
- Establish a coherent light arc: warm village morning, uncertain forest, cold cottage danger, and changed dawn.
- Use a recurring visual motif that evolves across the whole film—for example a red thread, path curve, leaf shape, basket weave, or another authored idea.
- Typography and captions must be integrated into the film's visual language, not placed in generic UI cards.
- Keep prose beyond the required lines minimal.
- Take a specific art-direction position. Avoid generic gradient backgrounds, stock dashboard aesthetics, emoji, clip-art shorthand, and interchangeable flat vector scenes.

## Playback experience

- Provide Play/Pause, Replay, 0.5×/1×/2× speed, captions toggle, and a scrubber with 12 clickable sequence markers.
- Controls must be discreet, keyboard accessible, touch friendly, and fade visually when unused without becoming impossible to recover.
- Support Space for Play/Pause, Left/Right for a short seek, and Home for replay.
- Any manual timeline action may take over from autoplay, but deterministic playback must remain available.
- Replay must restore seed `1812`, time 0, autoplay, and all visual state.
- Adapt composition to desktop and narrow mobile viewports without cropping away essential action.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by simplifying camera motion and transitions while preserving the complete 112-second narrative and exact sequence boundaries.
- Keep the console free of avoidable errors.

## Determinism

All story state, visual variation, motion, cuts, particles, weather, and procedural illustration must be reproducible from a supplied seed and exact timeline time. Animation must derive from the deterministic film clock, not accumulated display refresh frames.

Use fixed-step or absolute-time evaluation, cap wall-clock deltas, and handle tab visibility changes without skipping story state. Seeking to the same millisecond with the same seed and viewport must reproduce the same meaningful scene state.

## Deterministic film interface

Expose `window.__RED_HOOD_FILM__` with callable methods:

- `reset(seed)` — restore time 0 and all film state with the supplied integer seed;
- `play()` — continue deterministic playback;
- `pause()` — stop film-time advancement;
- `seek(milliseconds)` — render the exact state from 0 through 112000;
- `seekScene(index)` — seek to the start of a sequence using zero-based indices 0 through 11;
- `setSpeed(multiplier)` — accept 0.5, 1, or 2;
- `setCaptions(enabled)` — show or hide captions without changing film state;
- `getShotList()` — return the 12 sequence names, indices, start times, and end times;
- `snapshot()` — return a JSON-safe summary containing seed, playing, speed, film time, duration, sequence index, sequence name, progress, captions state, completion state, and meaningful character/scene state;
- `runSelfTest()` — exercise the real film functions and return `{ passed, tests }`.

The self-test must genuinely verify deterministic reset, the exact 112000 ms duration, all 12 ordered sequence boundaries, exact seeking, `seekScene`, pause, replay, default autoplay with seed 1812, end-state reachability, and finite numeric state. After testing, restore the normal opening state: seed 1812, time 0, playing at 1×.

## Technical constraints

- Create exactly one complete `index.html` file.
- Put all markup, styles, generated illustration, rendering, animation, editing, captions, controls, and logic in that file.
- Do not load external libraries, fonts, images, SVG files, videos, audio, APIs, JSON, or other network assets.
- Use only application-generated graphics and system fonts.
- The film must open directly in a modern browser without a server or build step.
- Do not embed copyrighted film stills, traced commercial illustrations, or external base64 assets.
- Every visible control must work.

## Quality bar

The result should feel like an independent animation studio, children's-book illustrator, cinematographer, production designer, editor, and creative coder made one cohesive miniature film together. Every sampled moment should look composed, but the work must become more powerful through time, continuity, and transformation.

Build the film directly without explaining the concept first. Run one concise validation pass across page load, autoplay, all 12 sequence boundaries, seeking, replay, responsive layout, deterministic snapshots, and the real self-test. Fix blocking failures, then keep the final response brief.
