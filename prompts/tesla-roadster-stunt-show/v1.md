<reference-facts>
These facts come from the footage and the coverage of the reveal. Use them.

- Location: SpaceX rocket test site, McGregor, Texas, at night. Pitch-black sky, flat dark tarmac, floodlight banks on tall poles, thin red LED light strips running along the ground and along the ramp edges, steel truss structures, power lines, haze.
- Downforce: fans in the floor pull the car to the surface, up to about 2 g. This is what lets it drive up the ramp and stay on it upside down.
- Thrusters: cold-gas (compressed air) jets borrowed from SpaceX attitude control. Main thrusters at the rear, attitude thrusters around the body. The jets are pale cyan-white, conical, short, and dissipate within a few metres. They are not fire.
- 0-60 mph in 0.81 seconds with the rear thrusters.
- Kickflip: from a rolling start, the car blasts off on downward jets, clears a Tesla Semi and four other Tesla cars parked side by side, does a full barrel roll in the air on its side jets, and lands level on the far side.
- Ramp: on-screen telemetry seen from above the car reads downforce rising from about 3,730 kg to 4,434 kg and pitch rising from 89.7° to about 115° as the car climbs past vertical.
- Landings and launches throw up large white smoke clouds, tinted red by the strip lights.
</reference-facts>

<simulation>
The stunts must come out of a simulation, not keyframed car positions.

- The car is a rigid body with mass and rotational inertia, integrated on a fixed timestep with a cap on steps per frame.
- Forces: gravity, tire traction and drive force, suspension contact with the ground and the ramp surface, fan downforce pressing the car onto whatever surface it is driving on, and the force of each thruster applied at its nozzle position so off-centre jets create torque.
- A flight controller decides drive throttle and each nozzle's thrust from the car's state to perform each stunt.
- Every jet's length and brightness follows the thrust that nozzle is producing at that moment.
- No randomness in the physics. The same settings give the same result on every replay. Seed any visual randomness, such as smoke.
- Slow motion changes the clock, not the result.
</simulation>
