I wrote the crash experiment to `index.html`. It runs without errors in a headless browser, but I haven't watched it in a real, interactive browser. The title and heading are both "Claude Sonnet 5.5".

I tried default settings plus several extremes: a heavy wall at low speed, the largest wall at top speed, and the tiniest wall. All ran without NaNs or errors, and simulating 8 seconds took about 0.2–0.4 s. Running the default test twice gave an identical outcome.

- **Default result:** at the defaults the car destroys the wall. Every brick is displaced and some are thrown up to about 79 m. This is expected, because the bricks are light (5 kg each once the two depth layers are counted) next to a 1,250 kg car.
- **Heavy walls:** a heavy wall (5 layers, 12 kg bricks) at low speed partly survives, and the car recoils. At 5 m/s against the largest wall, only 17 of 384 bricks are displaced.
- **Depth layers:** the simulation is 2D, so each brick's mass is multiplied by the number of depth layers. On screen the layers show as a small 3D extrusion behind each brick.
- **Car run-up:** the car covers a fixed 16 m run-up and reaches the chosen speed at impact. Its engine pushes through the centre of mass, which keeps it from flipping at high speed. That means it accelerates very hard at the top speeds. After impact the wheels lock and skid.

Besides Test, Reset, the settings panel and the telemetry, I added a playback-speed selector (1×, 0.5×, 0.25×, 0.1×), a "Restore defaults" button, and Space/R keyboard shortcuts. Changing a setting rebuilds the scene straight away. There is also a small `window.__sim` hook that I used for the scripted checks.
