# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Professional product designers, interface engineers, and creative technologists working on dense visual files. They need a fast, keyboard-first canvas for composing interface concepts, diagrams, and reusable design systems without leaving the browser.

## Product Purpose

Provide a credible single-user vector design workspace with a real scene graph, precise transforms, structured layers, inspectable properties, durable history, and portable files. Success means a fluent user can draw, select, transform, organize, style, persist, and export real compositions without encountering demo-only dead ends.

## Brand Personality

Precise, focused, quietly confident. The interface should recede behind the work, communicate state immediately, and reward practiced keyboard use.

## Anti-references

Avoid tutorial-canvas aesthetics, oversized controls, decorative dashboards, glass panels, novelty tool metaphors, ambiguous icon-only actions, and fake properties that do not affect the canvas. Avoid imitating Figma branding; category familiarity should come from interaction quality and density instead.

## Design Principles

1. The canvas is the product: chrome stays dense, stable, and subordinate to the document.
2. Every visible control is honest: it either changes selected content, explains why it is unavailable, or is omitted.
3. Precision remains discoverable: direct manipulation, numeric input, shortcuts, and contextual feedback all reach the same result.
4. State is legible at a glance: selection, tool mode, lock state, hierarchy, snapping, save status, and zoom never rely on guesswork.
5. Fast paths stay fast under load: pointer interactions avoid React render loops and scene queries avoid full scans.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for application chrome, full keyboard navigation for commands and properties, visible focus, reduced-motion support, non-color status cues, minimum 40px hit targets where density permits, and descriptive labels for icon controls. Canvas manipulation remains pointer-first but has numeric and keyboard equivalents.
