# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who want to understand current seismic activity at a glance, including curious readers, travelers, educators, journalists, and anyone checking whether a felt event appears in the live record. They may arrive on a desktop for broad exploration or on a phone for a fast local check.

## Product Purpose

Turn live USGS earthquake data into an accurate, explorable world view. Success means every event is visible at its true coordinates, broad patterns are legible, filters are useful, and selecting an event quickly answers where, when, how strong, and how deep it was.

## Brand Personality

Observant, precise, composed. The experience should feel like a beautifully maintained field instrument: information-rich without alarmism, technical without becoming clinical, and distinctive without decoration competing with the map.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic admin dashboards, sci-fi command centers, disaster sensationalism, decorative mock data, clustered map pins, glass panels layered everywhere, and map experiences that hide key controls or details behind ambiguous icons.

## Design Principles

- Let geography lead: the world map is the primary reading surface.
- Encode meaning twice: color, size, labels, and text work together so no insight depends on color alone.
- Preserve the individual event: each earthquake remains visible, selectable, and tied to its USGS record.
- Reward both scanning and inspection: global patterns should be immediate, while event details remain one action away.
- Stay calm under live conditions: loading, partial data, empty results, and network failures always have clear, useful states.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-operable controls and event selection, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, generous touch targets, readable map alternatives through the event list, and redundant magnitude/depth encoding for common color-vision differences.
