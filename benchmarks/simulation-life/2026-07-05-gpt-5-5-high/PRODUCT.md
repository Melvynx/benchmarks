# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People exploring artificial-life dynamics in a browser: they are watching a petri dish, changing ecological rules, and reading live evidence from charts while the run unfolds.

## Product Purpose

This simulator lets a user steer a predator-prey-food ecosystem and observe emergent population behavior. Success means the world behaves like an energy economy, not a screensaver: starvation, reproduction cost, predation, delayed regrowth, oscillation, collapse, and recovery must be visible and measurable.

## Brand Personality

Scientific, alive, precise. The interface should feel like an instrument panel attached to a living specimen: dense enough for experimentation, clear enough to trust, and vivid enough to watch behavior emerge.

## Anti-references

Avoid decorative random-dot simulations, fake chart data, generic game HUD styling, glossy sci-fi chrome, and marketing-page composition. The round rotating map view should clarify pursuit, clustering, tree-based food, and selection with readable cute oval characters, not distract from the model.

## Design Principles

- Show the organism first: the dish is the focal surface and the controls support it.
- Let data prove the story: charts and summaries must come from recorded simulation history.
- Preserve scientific tension: presets and controls should expose both coexistence and collapse.
- Make steering immediate: sliders and buttons should affect the live run without reloads.
- Prefer readable density: compact panels, stable metrics, and consistent controls over decorative space.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic controls, keyboard-focusable buttons and inputs, high-contrast labels, and responsive layouts without horizontal overflow. Motion should be meaningful to state and simulation, with CSS transitions kept short and nonessential animation minimized.
