A small world, real momentum.

The car accelerates along a powered test track to the selected speed. Its launcher disengages at the first brick contact. From there, collision impulses, gravity and ground friction determine the outcome.

Every brick is an independent rigid body. Staggered courses are dry stacked, with no mortar or artificial anchors. Depth is modeled as parallel physical slices: bricks collide within their own layer; the car and the ground interact with every layer. The side view offsets the layers slightly so you can see the thickness.

This is a two-dimensional rigid-body approximation. The car can pitch, slide and recoil; the bodywork's crumple is visual feedback. It is an experiment, rather than an automotive safety prediction.

Convex collision detection · clipped contact manifolds · accumulated normal and friction impulses · angular inertia · warm starting · position correction · adaptive substeps · contact sleeping. Dust never affects the physics.
