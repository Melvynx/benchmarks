The idea in brief: an air conditioner moves heat instead of making cold. Refrigerant circulates in a sealed copper loop. In Cooling, the cold indoor coil absorbs heat from room air; the compressor makes the refrigerant hotter than outdoor air, so the outdoor coil can release that heat outside. An expansion device drops the pressure so the refrigerant becomes cold again.

In Heating, a four-way reversing valve swaps the coils' roles: the outdoor coil becomes colder than winter air and absorbs heat, and the indoor coil releases it into the room. Indoor and outdoor air never mix. A split system uses two units joined by refrigerant lines; a monoblock keeps the whole circuit indoors and ducts outdoor air through the wall.
