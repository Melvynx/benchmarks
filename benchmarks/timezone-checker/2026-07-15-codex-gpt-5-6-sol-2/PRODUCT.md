# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Remote workers, distributed teams, and digital nomads comparing working hours across several locations. They usually arrive with a concrete scheduling question and need to understand viable windows in seconds, often between meetings or while coordinating asynchronously.

## Product Purpose

Make cross-timezone availability immediately legible. The product aligns every selected city against one reference day, exposes local hours without mental arithmetic, and distinguishes group-wide meeting windows from one-to-one availability against a base city.

## Brand Personality

Focused, calm, precise. The interface should feel dependable and quietly human, with clear language and confident data presentation.

## Anti-references

Avoid ornamental world-clock dashboards, dense enterprise scheduling software, generic card-heavy SaaS layouts, dark “mission control” styling, and prototype-like grids that require a legend before they make sense.

## Design Principles

1. Make alignment the hero: the hour matrix is the primary product surface and must scan cleanly in both orientations.
2. Preserve context: every local hour remains visible while overlap states receive unmistakable emphasis.
3. Prefer direct manipulation: changing mode, reference city, city list, or availability updates the answer immediately.
4. Explain outcomes compactly: summaries translate the grid into ranges without competing with it.
5. Design for repeated daily use: familiar controls, restrained motion, durable persistence, and no unnecessary setup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus, high contrast, reduced-motion support, semantic control labels, and state differences that do not rely on color alone. Preserve usability at narrow mobile widths and with long translated city or country names.
